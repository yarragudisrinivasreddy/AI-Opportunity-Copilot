# Benchmark processes (synthetic)

All invented for testing. Use as the text description input. Do not edit the text.

## P01. Garment unit: fabric lot inspection

We receive 40 to 60 fabric rolls a week. A checker pulls 10 percent of each lot, marks defects on a paper four-point sheet and the supervisor adds the points by calculator. Lots that fail go to a corner and the merchandiser is told by phone. Cutting plans are then written by hand on ledger paper using whatever lots passed. Mistakes show up when a bundle reaches stitching.

## P02. Pharma packaging: batch record entries

Operators write batch details, line clearance and quantities in a printed batch record. QA reviews every page at the end of the shift and returns pages with missing signatures. Corrections must follow the rules for strike-through and initials. A full batch can need three rounds of review before release.

## P03. Auto-component shop: machine downtime

Each of the 18 CNC machines has a handwritten downtime sheet. Operators write start time, stop time and a reason in their own words. On Saturdays one person types everything into Excel and builds a weekly utilisation chart. Reasons are inconsistent, so the maintenance head cannot see the top causes.

## P04. Rice mill: weighbridge and farmer payments

Trucks are weighed in and out, and the clerk writes the slip by hand in triplicate. Moisture is checked with a handheld meter and noted on the slip. At the end of the day the accountant computes the payable amount per farmer from rate cards in a diary and prepares payments. Disputes about weight or moisture are common.

## P05. Packaging printer: artwork approval

Customers send artwork on WhatsApp and email. A designer edits and sends versions back. Several versions float around and sometimes the wrong file goes to plate making. The production manager checks the final file against the approved one by eye, comparing colours, text and barcode.

## P06. Electronics assembly: board visual check

After soldering, inspectors look at each board under a lamp and magnifier for bridges, missing parts and lifted pads. Defects are tallied on a paper sheet. About 600 boards a day pass through and fatigue causes missed defects. Customers sometimes return boards for faults that were visible.

## P07. Tool room: quotation preparation

Customers send drawings and a target quantity. A senior engineer reads the drawing, estimates material, machining hours and heat treatment, and writes a quote in Word. Each quote takes up to two days, so only the senior engineer can do it. Quotes for repeat parts are written from scratch each time.

## P08. Warehouse: cycle counting

Once a week staff walk the racks with paper count sheets and write the physical count. The sheets are given to the store in-charge who compares them to the system report by hand and passes differences to the manager. Recounts happen days later, by which time stock has moved.

## P09. Dairy collection centre: milk quality tests

Farmers bring milk twice a day. The operator tests fat and SNF on a machine, reads the display and writes the values with the farmer name and quantity in a register. Payment statements every ten days are calculated by hand from the register. Farmers complain about errors and delays.

## P10. Furniture workshop: measurement to cutting list

The sales person measures a customer's room and sketches a wardrobe on paper. The carpenter converts the sketch into a cutting list by hand and orders boards. Mistakes in panel sizes cause wasted boards. Customers see no drawing until work has started.
